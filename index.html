How This App Works ✕

Where the data comes from

One published Google Sheet holds five tabs: ATNYC, AT, and Alt (per-identity master logs of filed complaints), plus shared Requested and Paid tabs that both carry a Dataset column (spelled inconsistently across tabs — "ATNYC" vs "AT NYC" — so this app normalizes spacing/case before matching).

Why the app still enriches live from NYC, even though the sheet already has some OATH fields

  • The sheet's snapshot can go stale — a case can flip from DEFAULTED to PAID IN FULL after the sheet was last refreshed. The app re-checks NYC's public OATH data for every summons number and overwrites the cached record when something changed.
  • Any difference it finds versus the previous snapshot gets logged in the Changes tab — new complaints, hearing-status changes, hearing-result changes, payment-amount changes.

How records get merged

Within each identity, rows are joined by Summons Number: master record + matching Paid/Requested entry (filtered to that identity via the Dataset column) + live NYC enrichment. The Home tab then merges all three identities into one sorted, searchable list.

Home tab status logic (exact rules from the original app, in priority order)

  • If the complaint's Status field literally says "Submitted", it's tagged Submitted (purple) — and this blocks the Paid/Requested tags from applying.
  • Otherwise a match in the Paid log makes it Paid (green); else a match in the Requested log makes it Requested (yellow).
  • Then, overriding all of the above: a Hearing Result containing "dismissed" forces Dismissed (dark red, white text); else a Status containing "disqualified" forces Disqualified (red).
  • Finally, at render time: if there's no Hearing Date and the row isn't Disqualified/Dismissed/Submitted, it displays light blue regardless of the above — "no hearing scheduled yet" takes visual precedence over payment status.
  • So the real priority order is: dismissed > disqualified > submitted > paid > requested. Disqualified and dismissed complaints always remain visible and counted.

The payout rule

Normally you receive 25% of what NYC collects. But if the paid/violation amount is over $1,000, the payout is capped at a flat $250 — this app applies that cap everywhere a payout is estimated or forecasted. Actual historical payouts on already-paid rows always come straight from your "Paid To Me" column, never the formula.

The Home stat cards

  • Pending Amount — expected payout summed across everything currently in Requested status (asked for, not yet received). Rows with no dollar amount on file assume the standard $87.50.
  • Paid + Pending — money in hand plus money actively in the pipeline.
  • Estimated Grand Total — money received, plus confirmed pending, plus every open complaint weighted by your real win rate and faded by age against your current conversion pace. A realistic forecast, not a ceiling.

Complaints without a summons yet

Filings DEP hasn't converted into summonses yet (blank Summons Number) are kept and counted — they display light blue with no payout, and they can't match the Paid/Requested logs or NYC data until a summons exists. When one is assigned, the Changes tab logs it as a "Summons Assigned" event.

Forecasts use recent data only

Both lags — request→paid and submission→hearing — have gotten dramatically worse over time, so lifetime averages would lowball reality. Forecasted payment dates use the average of your last 20 paid requests; estimated hearing dates (shown in italics for rows with no hearing yet, or as "overdue ~Xd" once the estimate has passed) use your last 50 submission→hearing gaps. Estimates are display-only and never feed the revenue math.

How Hours Worked / Hourly Rate is calculated

There's no real clock-in data, so hours are inferred from clip timestamps: a gap of 1.5 hours or more between two clips starts a new "shift." Each shift is padded 30 minutes on either end. Only main-shift sightings (weekdays, 5am-6pm) count toward hours — anything on a weekend or before 5am/after 6pm is pulled out and counted separately as an "overtime truck" instead of being time-clustered. Hourly rate = that day's estimated payout ÷ that day's main-shift hours. A handful of source rows have a corrupted clip end-time (a date typo, an AM/PM flip); anything implying more than an hour for a single clip is treated as unreliable for hours math and surfaced in New Money as worth double-checking with DEP, rather than allowed to silently inflate a day's hours.

Offense tiers

The Description column is scanned for "2nd offense" / "second offense" / "3rd offense" etc. Repeat offenses raise the fine ($350 / $450 / $600), and all expected-payout estimates use the right tier. First offense is assumed when nothing is mentioned.

Incident time, not submission time

The calendar and all Stats group by when the violation actually occurred (the clip's From timestamp), falling back to the submission date for old rows without one. The estimated-hearing-date model only trusts months where a majority of filings have actually been reviewed — the few early returns from a mostly-unreviewed month are biased fast and are ignored.

The grand formula (what every row is worth)

  • Paid — actual dollars received.
  • Requested / NYC shows Paid in Full — full base payout. Confirmed money.
  • Defaulted — a finding of violation exists; collection is roughly a coin-flip, so base × 0.5.
  • Hearing scheduled — demonstrably moving, so no age fade: base × your lifetime win rate W.
  • Open (waiting) — base × W × T(age), where T is your own conversion-tail curve, time-warped to today's slower pace: a 300-day-old row in a 2×-slower era is only "150 days along" and keeps most of its value, while a 2023 zombie is past the tail and fades to $0.
  • Disqualified / dismissed — $0.

W is measured lifetime-only on purpose: disqualifications resolve fast and wins resolve slow, so a "recent-only" win rate would be biased low. Hover the Estimated Grand Total card for the live per-category breakdown; the console logs it on every refresh.

Disqualification reasons

A sixth sheet tab maps Complaint Number → the specific disqualification reason (joined by complaint number, since disqualified filings never get a summons). Disqualified rows show the real reason wherever it appears.

Company names

A ~130-entry lookup table maps known messy spellings ("CONSOLIDATED EDISON COMPANY OF NY INC.", "CONED", …) to one canonical name, so search finds all variants of the same real company. The table displays the name exactly as filed; searching matches both the raw and the canonical name.
